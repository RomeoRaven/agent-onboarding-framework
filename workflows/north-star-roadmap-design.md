# North Star and roadmap design — portable method

Use when a company, product, site, project, or substantial initiative needs a shared end direction and a high-level route before detailed execution. This is a conditional strategy method, not a startup requirement. Its result is an understandable North Star, observable end state, and staged roadmap—not a full implementation plan or a new task system.

## Owner and approval boundary

Find the scope's existing strategy and artifact owner first. Check any parent direction and any existing brief, vision, roadmap, or tracker. Preserve its original intent; revise the smallest adequate owner surface rather than create a peer vision document or duplicate execution queue. If the work would change parent strategy, priority order, a public promise, or another owner's governing direction, present the decision and obtain that owner's required approval before mutation. Drafting direction does not authorize project execution, deployment, publication, or changes to agent/board instructions.

A strategy-alignment owner, when one exists, decides hierarchy and artifact type. This method shapes the direction and high-level roadmap for the selected scope. A separate phase-planning/project owner translates an accepted phase into buildable action slices. Do not create that later layer here unless separately requested and authorized.

## Inputs

Gather only what affects the direction: the request and why it matters now; scope and existing owner; parent direction; primary audience/user and outcome; current promise or purpose; constraints, anti-promises, and approval gates; known assets, work, evidence, and unknowns; and the intended destination for the resulting brief. For a public-facing scope, include publication and preview boundaries. If parent direction or owner cannot be found and the choice materially changes the answer, stop at a read-only decision proposal rather than pretending the hierarchy is settled.

## Method

1. Frame one decision: What is the original intent, whose direction is being shaped, why now, what remains unchanged, and what must pause? Name the smallest durable artifact or existing section that could hold the answer. Compare the option of updating an existing owner against creating a new brief; choose the latter only when no suitable owner exists.
2. Express the North Star in one plain sentence: `<initiative> exists so <primary audience> can <valuable outcome> through <distinct approach>`. Add the audience order, promise, evidence, boundaries, and anti-promises needed to prevent misinterpretation. Treat the sentence as a testable proposal, not a slogan.
3. Describe an observable end state. What would a user experience, what capability or artifact would exist, what evidence would demonstrate it, and what must remain true under pressure? Mark assumptions and unresolved decisions separately from accepted facts. Choose dimensions suited to the scope rather than copying a site or product checklist.
4. Backcast from that end state into a few outcome pillars and ordered phases. For each phase, state why it is next, its phase-level done indicator, key dependency or decision, and what it deliberately defers. Keep implementation slices, dates, staffing, and full task breakdowns out unless they are essential to a decision now.
5. Forward-map current work against the proposed direction: use now, reshape, later, park, or reject. Identify the smallest coherent first phase that makes the direction usable or legible; neither a token step nor a premature whole-program build.
6. State a simple future-work test: which North Star and pillar does a candidate serve, what audience outcome or proof does it improve, and should it proceed now, be reshaped, parked, or rejected? Keep this test in the approved direction's owner rather than installing a new enforcement layer by default.
7. Present the draft direction, phase sequence, first-phase done indicator, tradeoffs, open decisions, and exact approval boundary for review. Only after direction and the first phase are accepted should their execution owner make a detailed phase blueprint or first action queue. Route follow-up to an existing tracker without duplicating it.

## Proportionate output

A brief or existing owner section should make the North Star, audience and promise, observable end state, constraints and anti-goals, evidence, parent fit, and unresolved choices visible. A high-level roadmap should contain outcome pillars, phase order and done indicators, key dependencies, the first coherent phase, and parked work. One artifact may hold both when that is simpler. If the operator asked only for a discussion or recommendation, return a no-mutation draft and decision rather than writing files.

## Validation and stop

Test the plan backward: if all proposed phases reach their done indicators, would the observable end state actually follow? Test it forward: do existing assets and the first phase make a plausible start? Check parent fit, owner and non-duplicate artifact choice, visible assumptions, phase boundaries, and any public or consequential approval gates. If the parent direction, owner, or key strategic choice is unresolved, do not claim the roadmap is accepted or turn it into action items. If the goal changes during review, recheck the original intent and stable end goal before rewriting the owner. Do not blueprint every future phase or publish a public-facing direction through this workflow.
